Section 1Waiting on you

Nothing here needs code. Ordered by what it costs you if it keeps waiting.

  • YouThe $9 coverage test — about an hour
    Fifty cards whose value you already know. Subscribe to thecardapi Starter, run them, count how many come back with a matched quote. The buy sheet and every judgement about comp quality wait behind that number. Their §4a explicitly permits SaaS use, local caching to serve your users, and derived analytics as your own IP — so the licence question is settled and this is purely "does the data actually cover what you buy".
  • YouThe cron interlock now has a price it did not have before
    CRON_SECRET is unset and the six jobs 401 by design. That was double-run protection while Master-Ops scheduled the same jobs — it stopped doing so on 25 July, so that justification is gone and only the hardening gate remains.

    What changed: the lookback tier clamps backfill, not accumulated depth. card_market_sales is append-only with dedup, so a 14-day window run daily becomes a year of history in a year. Every day nothing runs is history the cheap tier can no longer bank. Manual triggering is the middle path and needs no interlock decision at all.
  • YouOne paid hour of counsel, on one sentence
    Whether a user-uploaded Seller Hub export falls outside eBay's "eBay Content does not include information that you lawfully obtain independent of eBay." All pooling is downstream of it, and pool is false on every source until it is answered. Not urgent while you are the only user; unanswerable by engineering judgement.
  • YouStorage tier numbers
    The metered model is decided. Every screen that would warn someone before they hit a quota is written around a threshold nobody has chosen yet.
  • NoteYour working copy is four commits ahead of what I can see
    Attribution rendering, Terapeak/Seller Hub, the PriceCharting terms read, and a pre-call page are committed locally but not pushed. Your CLAUDE.md also references migration 20260751, which is not on main — only 20260750_sale_provenance is. Nothing here was merged or touched; this page reflects origin/main only.

Section 2Two questions closed

Both had been carried as open since the first version of this document.

  • DecidedPricing is metered, and it went your way
    "cardops is a tool in the form of an app/website that people will need to purchase computing power beyond their free amount for."
    Compute-metered with a free tier, over the research's recommended flat $50–150/mo. It fits what is already built — the credit ledger, per-run cost metering, per-photo byte accounting.

    Three consequences are code constraints, not marketing: catalogue lookups must never consume credits, including after an allowance runs out (Scryfall's terms forbid paywalling their data — the moment "out of credits" hides a card's name, it is behind a paywall); a hard spending stop rather than a soft one; and the bulk floor checked before any paid lookup, so a 5,000-card collection buy can never bill for 5,000 of them.
  • AnsweredSold comps are buyable, and the pass that said otherwise was wrong
    An earlier research pass concluded there was no reachable, permitted API for third-party sold comps and that pasting was therefore permanent — which reasonably read as the project being pointless. It asked what was available for free and reported that as the map of what exists. The API was already in the repo: thecardapi.ts, shipped 21 July, on a free tier whose 3-day window produces almost nothing.

    Verified against live pages on the 29th: Starter $9 (10,000 rows/day, 14-day lookback, 500k stored), Builder $49 (30 days), Pro $199 (90 days), Unlimited Lookback a $99 Pro-gated add-on. Start at $9, not $298 — the add-on buys the past now instead of waiting for it, and that is a decision to make after the coverage test, not before.

Section 4What to build, ranked

Ranked by value against effort. Re-ranked this run because the money engine shipped and unblocked the item that was sitting at the bottom.

#WhatEffort
1The buy sheet. Newly unblocked — it was #11 and gated on the money engine. Scan a stack, get a defensible offer: matched identities at a chosen haircut, bulk residual stated separately, days-to-sell, and an explicit band for how much rests on weak matches. Every other tool tells you what a card you own is worth; this tells you what to pay before you own it. Gated on the $9 coverage test — an offer built on comps that miss is worse than no offer.~3 weeks
2Resolve the PriceCharting licence. Their terms are internal-use-only and the connector is already wired. Under "Beau's tool" that was arguable; under "a product" it is not — and free does not save it, because the term is about who uses the data, not what it costs. Two clean paths: ask them for a redistribution quote (often an email, not a legal project), or restrict the connector to your own account. Cheap now, expensive after a second user exists.hours–days
3Cert verification against PSA. Intake already reads grader, grade and cert off the slab. GetByCertNumber is free and cert data never changes, so cache forever per identity. Converts a vision guess into a checked fact, and catches the one fraud that costs resellers money. Test the free rate limit with a live token before designing around volume; population fields return null.2–3 days
4A grading tier picker. The fee defaults are fixed — PSA 50 / BGS 30 / SGC 25 / CGC 25 + $12 ship, up from 25 / 22 / 18 / 18 + $8 — and the screen now renders your configured fee instead of a hardcoded "~$20" caption. What is still missing is which tier: PSA Economy $50 against Regular $79.99, with Value tiers paused since June. "Should I grade this" is tier-dependent and the app cannot ask the question.hours
5Year-end COGS close with a named exception list. Beginning inventory + purchases − ending inventory, partitioned by source, read through readAllSafe. The differentiator is the header: "debits = credits across 1,204 entries; 0 partial reads; 4 cards have no stated basis." Seasonal — the deadline is December and it is July.~1 week
6Free, complete export, stated as a promise. /cards/export exists. The commitment does not: a page that says it is complete and free forever, and a test proving it is not silently capped at 1000 rows. CSV export is paywalled across the entire category — for a one-man product against funded incumbents this is the most credible trust signal available, and it costs almost nothing.2–3 days
7Finish the grading loop. The midpoint bug is fixed — grade EV now works across the whole estimate rather than collapsing "PSA 8 to 10" into a single 9. What remains: card_grading_submissions exists and is dead. Extend it to order level — grader, tier, order #, declared value, ship date. PSA Value Bulk is 140–160 business days, making a submission the longest-duration position in the business, and the app cannot see it. Migration — never auto-merges.2–3 weeks
8Catalogue poisoning defences. Sales hang off the shared identity, so one bad paste or wash sale reaches every owner. No provenance weighting, no outlier quarantine, no dispute path — and card_id is nullable on delete set null with no added_by, so deleting a card severs the only link to who contributed a row. A poisoned sale can become permanently un-attributable. Cheapest while you are the only contributor — and the paste screen above is exactly what stops that being true.~1 week
9Bulk image ingest. A folder or ZIP of flatbed images, fronts paired to backs, through the existing vision and identity trigger. A phone rig tops out at 80–120 cards/hour against 250/hour overhead. A 5,000-card collection buy currently never enters the system, or enters as an unpriced lump. Identification-grade only, labelled as such — it can never back a condition claim, because the geometry is not there.2–3 weeks
10Review queue ranked by money at risk. Not a better model — triage. Surface the ambiguous /25 parallel first, auto-accept the base card, and when vision is torn between two identities name the discriminating attribute. Corrections write back to the shared catalog once, for everyone. The constraint is your hours, not model accuracy.1–2 weeks
11Surface the capture record + a dispute pack. Distance, angle, per-edge lock, sharpness and clipping are all measured and none of it is visible. Bundle a sold card's pre-ship set with timestamps and per-image sha256. Audit first: capture_meta writes from two call sites only and photo dimensions are unpopulated, so this renders blanks for most of the library today.1–2 weeks

Below the line, named so they are not forgotten: card-show offline mode (the offline write must queue as a proposed transaction, never an auto-post); eBay own-sales ingest for real fees and payouts (blocked on the RuName cutover — it would also give the money engine a fee rate derived from your settled sales rather than a published schedule, which is the better input); inbound consignment (months, and now a scheduling call rather than an identity one).

Section 5Your queue

Your asks, in the order you made them, never re-ranked. Every one so far turned out to be partly built already.

  • DoneQ1 — the intake session card list
    The cards you book during a session stay on screen. As predicted it was a wiring job: card_intake_sessions and card_intake_items had existed since day one with zero references anywhere. The open question resolved in favour of one component with more actions enabled at the confirm step, rather than a separate review screen.
  • OpenQ2 — "Add to Group" in the photograph step
    The API is done: create, rename, delete, add, remove, all RLS'd. Only the dropdown is missing. Your answer — sticky, not session-scoped — was better than either option offered: a value carries forward from the card where you set it, every card keeps whatever was current when it was captured, and changing it mid-stack affects only what comes after. That is how a box actually gets worked through.

    It generalises past groups: every field gets a clear control to its right, because a sticky value needs a way to say "not this one" that is distinct from "I haven't chosen yet". Three states per field, and they need to look different: inherited, set here, deliberately cleared.
  • OpenQ3 — a Card Groups destination page
    Tables, RLS, full CRUD and filtering all exist. What is missing is a page where a group is the thing you are looking at. Q4's answer unblocked this by settling what a group is.
  • AnsweredQ4 — the objective hierarchy is a query, not a group
    Sport, year, brand and set are already columns. A hierarchy over them needs no membership table, no assignment step and no maintenance, and it is always correct because it is derived — fix a card's year and it moves in the tree by itself. It also dissolved the inheritance question entirely: each level is a filter, so a card in Baseball › 2025 › Topps › Chrome is trivially in all four.

    Do not fix the order. "Everything Pokémon" and "everything from 2025" and "all my Prizm regardless of year" want different trees. Yours is the default; the facets are reorderable. Groups become purely user-created, and group-versus-tag is weight rather than cardinality — the test is whether you would ever reorder the cards inside it.
  • OpenQ5 — ownership is not a tag, and that is the important part
    Three things here and only two are labels. Facets (patch, auto, RC) are derived from card fields and should never become hand-typed — that is the same fact stored twice, disagreeing. Tags are adjectives you invent. Ownership is a fact with money attached.

    If a consignor's card is marked with a tag, deleting the tag silently absorbs someone else's property into your inventory value. It must not count in your net worth, must not have a basis, and its sale is a payout obligation rather than revenue — and the marketplace still reports the consignor's full sale price as your 1099-K gross. That is a double-entry problem, which is precisely why competitors ship "consignment tracking" without solving it.

    A day's work that stops the worst outcome: a nullable owned_by that simply excludes the card from every total. Honest, and it buys time before full consignment accounting.

Section 7What not to build

Each of these was considered and rejected for a reason worth keeping written down.

  • NoAn accuracy race against eBay's comps
    They shipped a free camera-scan price guide backed by two years of their own transactions in March. You cannot out-accurate the company that owns the transactions. Checkable is the winnable axis — provenance, basis, and what you keep. Scanning must be good enough not to lose the deal; it can never be why you win.
  • NoA pooled per-user execution model
    New this run. Completed sales are public and pooled — your call, and it is the network effect. But "does this seller realise above or below market, and how fast" is not a fact about the card; it encodes photo quality, feedback score, shipping speed, patience and return policy. Pooling it renders one prolific seller's behaviour as "the market" and hands a beginner with three feedback the numbers of a top-rated store. Market data pooled, execution data never.
  • NoA marketplace, or holding anyone's money or cards
    Competitors' worst reviews are payment and shipping disputes, and those reviewers abandon the tracker too.
  • NoA sales-tax engine, or any tax package that computes tax
    Jurisdictional quicksand. No rate applied, no form line numbers as headers, no ordering by tax advantage, no short/long label — raw day counts, and classification shown as "as classified by you on <date>: <reason>." Record and flag; never compute an obligation.
  • NoGrade estimates shown to a buyer
    Internal EV is the entire point. The moment "likely PSA 9" appears on a showcase it is a representation to a buyer about a third party's future act, using PSA's marks. Every card that comes back lower is a dispute.
  • NoProjected future value, or "cards as an asset class"
    Also a dead commercial category: Collectable wound down Nov 2024, Dibbs Mar 2023, Mythic Markets 2021. Report what happened, never what will.
  • NoSet registry, census, card-show POS, or a cheap casual tier
    A registry needs a catalog of what exists; yours is a catalog of what has been seen. POS filled in 2025–26 with a free entrant — capturing show-day cash into the ledger is the part worth having. And a casual tier drags the roadmap toward features a dealer does not need.

Section 8Posture

These constrain every recommendation above and outrank all of them.

Nothing is what it isn't

No figure or image presented as something it is not. Grading evidence traces to what came off the sensor. A surface map is evidence and must never be published as a product photo.

A licence is code

Every source declares persist / redisplay / pool rights, and the write path consults them. An unknown source id is denied by default — "we never checked" must not read the same as "permitted".

Never median across price bases

eBay quotes what the buyer paid; auction houses quote hammer with a premium still to come. A venue whose premium cannot be cited is excluded, and the exclusion is reported.

Break-even is the fee equation inverted

Never basis plus a percentage — fees are charged on the higher price too, and the naive version says an offer clears when it loses money. No basis stated means "no stated basis", never a floor of $0.

Nothing provisional is stored

The accumulator ignores duplicates, so a fast-settle estimate written once can never be corrected. Store the settled figure or store nothing.

Catalogue lookups stay free

Including after an allowance is exhausted. The moment "out of credits" hides a card's name, Scryfall's data is behind a paywall their terms forbid.

Tax is recorded, never determined

The app stores your call and your stated reason. Bookkeeping hygiene and flags to raise with a CPA — never filing, never advice.

Money moves on a human decision

Nothing posts to real books from a cron. Merges and deletions that would lose work get surfaced rather than done.

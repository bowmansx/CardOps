**Downstream**

**Working method**

## 3. MUST DO

1. **Autograph contamination.** `card_fingerprint()` omits `is_auto`/`is_relic`
   and nothing filters them downstream, so a signed and an unsigned copy share
   one identity **and one pooled cross-tenant sales history**. Blast radius is
   one user today and grows with every account; fixing it later means splitting
   identities and re-partitioning accumulated history. **Needs a migration.**
2. **PSA fee default is $25; reality is ~$100.** Grade-EV is giving wrong
   advice today and errs toward "grade it".
3. **A backup script.** `pg_dump` doesn't touch the storage bucket, so the
   photos have no copy anywhere. Cheapest possible moment to fix is now, while
   inventory is nearly empty.
4. **Merge the two branches.**

## 4. SHOULD DO

- **Capture modes: Add / Find / Update.** Photographing a card currently means
  only "create a new one". Splitting identification from creation turns four of
  Beau's requests into one pipeline. `FIND` is useful alone.
- **The grading round trip.** Batch FIND → declared value → tier → fee →
  `at_grader` (blocked from selling) → batch UPDATE on return with grade, cert
  and the fee as a cost line. `card_grading_submissions` already exists in the
  schema with **zero code references**.
- **eBay purchase ingestion.** Basis is born on the buy side and every existing
  eBay route is sell-side. The documented failure mode is abandonment, not
  absence — people quit because entry is manual.
- **Import column mapping.** Beau's own `00-what-cardops-is` names format
  ingestion as a native goal; the import path has fixed headers and never reads
  `card_format_profiles`. This is the switching cost.
- **Confidence gate.** Confidence is captured and shown; nothing gates on it. A
  card identified at 40% can be listed on eBay.
- **Reservation, not quantity sync.** Every card is quantity-1.
- **Watchlist** — `card_watchlist` exists and is dead code.
- **Scan-correction capture** — "AI said X, I corrected to Y" is unrecoverable
  if not recorded at the time.

## 6. SHOULD BE CONSIDERING — surfaced but not yet on Beau's radar

- **Relative-FMV allocation for lump-sum buys.** IRS Pub 551 prescribes
  allocating one payment across multiple assets by relative fair value. The flat
  running average is right for bulk commons and wrong for a $10k collection
  holding one $2,000 rookie and 900 commons. **Beau's own cost-basis note is
  groping toward exactly this** — "automatic adjustment based on the estimated
  value of a card" IS relative-FMV allocation. Same primitive covers box breaks.
- **Consignment as accounting, not tracking.** The marketplace 1099-Ks the
  consignor's full sale price as your gross. Fixing that needs the sale booked
  as revenue and the payout as an expense line — a double-entry problem, which
  is why competitors ship consignment tracking and still can't solve it.
- **The wedge is books, not basis.** Cost basis stopped being a differentiator
  around 2025. Per-card inventory closing into a real double-entry ledger,
  across multiple legal entities, is what none of ~15 products attempt.
- **Multi-entity is rare and unadvertised.** Zero of eight products checked let
  one operator keep two businesses apart.
- **Data survival as a marketing asset.** This market carries real precedent for
  apps dying and taking data with them. Free unmetered export plus a CPA-ready
  year file is a trust position worth stating — undercut today by no self-serve
  deletion and no full-account archive.
- **1099-K reverted to $20,000 / 200 transactions.** Most serious sellers now
  get **no form at all**, so their own records ARE the tax record. That makes
  the books stronger, not less necessary — and any messaging built on $600 is
  wrong.
- **What to avoid:** competing on scanning or price guides (eBay gives both
  away free now), a marketplace or holding money, card-show POS, a sales-tax
  engine, set registry, and chasing the casual collector.
- **The tension worth resolving.** The research says the winnable market is
  narrow and rich — dealers who've become businesses — priced at $50–150/mo.
  Beau wants metered pricing, mostly free. These converge more than they
  conflict: metering charges the volume dealer and costs the hobbyist nothing.
  But it needs numbers before P4 can be built.
